SBA Express loans: limits, speed and who should use them

SBA Express is the SBA's quick-decision version of the 7(a) loan. It goes up to $500,000, can be a line of credit, and your own bank makes the call without waiting for SBA review. The trade-off: SBA only backs half the loan, so lenders want a solid borrower.

Checked November 20, 2026

The short answer

  • Limit: $500,000.
  • SBA guarantee: 50% of the loan, versus 75% to 85% on other 7(a) loans.
  • Why it's faster: Express lenders "have delegated authority to process, close, service, and liquidate the 7(a) loan without SBA review." The lender decides, using mostly its own forms.
  • Lines of credit: allowed, for up to 10 years. Standard 7(a) and 7(a) Small loans can't be revolving lines.
  • Best for: an established business with good credit that already banks with an Express lender and needs a working capital line or a loan under $500,000.

How SBA Express works

Like every 7(a) loan, an Express loan comes from a bank or other lender, not from the SBA. The SBA guarantees part of it, which lets the lender offer longer terms or say yes to a business it would otherwise turn down.

What makes Express different is who decides. With other 7(a) loans, unless the lender has its own delegated authority, the SBA reviews the loan before it is approved. Under Express, SBA's lender page says the credit decision is "Made by the lender", and the lender "primarily uses own forms and procedures, plus SBA Form 1919" (the SBA borrower information form). In practice it feels much like applying for a regular bank loan.

The catch is the smaller guarantee. On a regular 7(a) loan over $150,000, the SBA guarantees 75% of the loan. On Express it guarantees 50%. With half the risk on its own books, the lender will judge you closer to its normal credit standards.

All figures from SBA's lender page. Term loans follow the usual 7(a) limits: ten years or less unless the loan pays for real estate or equipment with a longer useful life, and up to 25 years for real estate.

Collateral: per SBA, "Lenders are not required to take collateral for loans up to $50,000." Above that, the lender follows its own collateral policy, but a loan isn't supposed to be declined only because collateral falls short. Expect a personal guarantee and a lien on business assets on larger loans; ask the lender what it will want.

Who qualifies: the same as any 7(a) loan. Per SBA, the business must operate for profit, be located in the U.S., be small under SBA size standards, not be an ineligible business type, be unable to get credit elsewhere on reasonable terms, and be creditworthy with a reasonable ability to repay.

Interest rates

You and the lender negotiate the rate, but it "may not exceed the SBA maximum". For variable-rate loans the maximum is a base rate (usually the prime rate) plus a margin that shrinks as the loan grows:

Loan size Highest variable rate $50,000 or less Base rate + 6.5% $50,001 to $250,000 Base rate + 6.0% $250,001 to $350,000 Base rate + 4.5% Over $350,000 Base rate + 3.0%

Source: SBA 7(a) terms and conditions. The bank prime rate was 7.00% in the Federal Reserve's November 19, 2026 release. These are ceilings, not quotes: a strong borrower should be offered less.

Fees

Most 7(a) loans, Express included, carry an upfront SBA guarantee fee charged on the guaranteed part of the loan. The lender pays it to SBA and usually passes it to you. For loans that get an SBA loan number from October 1, 2026 (fiscal year 2027), as summarized by the lenders' trade group NAGGL from SBA Information Notice 5000-881797:

Loan size (over 12 months) Upfront fee on guaranteed part $150,000 or less 2% $150,001 to $700,000 3% 12 months or less 0.25%

Two exceptions worth knowing. NAGGL reports a 0% upfront fee, by statute, for SBA Express loans to veteran-owned businesses. And loans of $700,000 or less to manufacturers, certain food supply chain businesses and businesses in rural areas also carry a 0% upfront fee in fiscal 2027. The lender also pays SBA an annual service fee of 0.55% of the guaranteed balance, and that one cannot be charged to the borrower.

Worked example: two Express loans

These loans are made up for illustration, using the prime rate above. Your lender will set the real rate and fee.

A. $150,000 revolving line for working capital Highest rate: 7.00% prime + 6.0% = 13.00% Guaranteed part: $150,000 × 50% = $75,000 Upfront fee: $75,000 × 2% = $1,500 B. $400,000 term loan for equipment, 10 years Highest rate: 7.00% prime + 3.0% = 10.00% Guaranteed part: $400,000 × 50% = $200,000 Upfront fee: $200,000 × 3% = $6,000 Monthly payment at 10.00%, 120 mo. = $5,286.03 (Same loan as a standard 7(a), 75%: $300,000 × 3% fee = $9,000)

Two things stand out. The Express fee is lower than a same-size standard 7(a), because only half is guaranteed. And loan B's payment is about $63,400 a year, so the lender will want to see earnings that cover it with room to spare. Who should use SBA Express

  • Good fit: a profitable business with a few years of history and clean books, that needs $500,000 or less, and wants a line of credit or a faster yes from a bank it already knows.
  • Good fit: a business that a bank likes but won't lend to on its own terms, for example because it wants a longer term than the bank's normal loan.
  • Look elsewhere if you need more than $500,000: a standard 7(a), or a 504 for buildings and big equipment.
  • Look elsewhere if your credit is thin: because SBA covers only half, a lender may decline an Express loan it would make as a 7(a) Small loan with an 85% guarantee, or you may be better served by an SBA Microloan or a community lender.
  • Contractors and seasonal businesses needing a line tied to specific jobs or seasons should also ask about CAPLines.

How to apply

  1. Ask your own bank first whether it makes SBA Express loans. Not every SBA lender does. SBA's free Lender Match can connect you with others.
  2. Know your numbers. Lenders test whether your earnings cover the new payment; see our DSCR guide. Plan the timing with the free 13-week cash flow template.
  3. Bring the usual package: tax returns, recent financial statements, a debt schedule and what the money is for. Our loan application checklist lists it all.
  4. Compare offers. Ask two or three lenders for the rate, the fee, the term and the collateral they want, and check each against the caps above.

This guide is general information, not financial or legal advice. SBA rules, limits, rates and fees change, so confirm the details with your lender or SBA before you apply.
