The free money checklist for small businesses

Thirteen grants, tax credits, refunds and rebates owners can claim, plus two to watch. Tick off each one that fits, then take the list to your accountant.

Free money here means money you don't pay back. Loans aren't on this list. Amounts are as each official source states them. This is general information, not tax or legal advice, and rules can change, so confirm with your accountant before you claim.

Tip: print this page or save it as a PDF from your browser.

Tax credits

Credits cut your tax bill dollar for dollar. You claim them when you file, so the first step is telling your accountant which ones apply.

01Retirement plan startup credits

How much
Up to $5,000 a year for the first 3 years, plus up to $1,000 per employee in contributions, plus $500 a year if the plan auto-enrolls people
Who
Businesses with 100 or fewer employees (paid $5,000+) that start a new retirement plan, such as a 401(k), SIMPLE IRA or SEP, with at least one non-highly-paid employee in it
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8881 when you file. The contribution credit is 100% of what you put in for each employee (up to $1,000) in years one and two, then steps down, and doesn't apply to employees earning over about $100,000.
Note
If you have 50 or fewer employees, the startup credit covers 100% of eligible setup costs.

Source: IRS: retirement plan startup costs tax credit

02Small business health care credit

How much
Up to 50% of the health insurance premiums you pay, for two years in a row
Who
Fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent employees, average wages under the IRS limit (adjusted each year), you pay at least half of employee-only premiums, and the plan comes through the SHOP Marketplace
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8941.
Note
The credit shrinks as headcount and average wages rise, so check the numbers with your accountant.

Source: IRS: small business health care tax credit

03Employer child care credit (bigger from 2026)

How much
50% of qualified child care costs, up to $600,000 a year
Who
Businesses with average yearly gross receipts of $32 million or less over the past five years. Larger businesses get 40%, up to $500,000
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8882. Counts money you spend providing or paying for child care for employees, plus 10% of resource and referral costs.
Note
This took effect for tax years starting in 2026.

Source: IRS: employer-provided child care credit, 2026 and later

04Paid family and medical leave credit

How much
12.5% to 25% of the wages you pay employees on family or medical leave, up to 12 weeks a year
Who
Employers with a written policy giving at least two weeks of paid family and medical leave a year at 50% or more of normal wages. From 2026 it covers employees with six months of service and part-timers working 20+ hours a week
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8994. From 2026 you can base it on premiums for a paid leave insurance policy instead of wages.
Note
Congress made this credit permanent in 2025.

Source: Journal of Accountancy: IRS guidance on the expanded credit (Aug 2026) · IRS: Form 8994

05Disabled access credit

How much
Up to $5,000 a year: half of what you spend on accessibility between $250 and $10,250
Who
Businesses with $1 million or less in receipts or no more than 30 full-time employees last year
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8826 for things like ramps, accessible restrooms, signage or interpreters. Businesses of any size can also deduct up to $15,000 a year for removing barriers.

Source: IRS: tax benefits for businesses with employees with disabilities · IRS: Form 8826

06Research credit, paid against payroll tax

How much
Up to $500,000 a year off your payroll taxes
Who
Young businesses: under $5 million in gross receipts this year and no receipts more than five years back. Work that can count includes designing new products, prototypes, processes or software
How to get it
Elect it on IRS Form 6765 with your income tax return, then take it on Form 8974 with your quarterly payroll returns. It reduces payroll tax even if you don't owe income tax yet.
Note
Older or larger businesses can still claim the regular research credit against income tax.

Source: IRS: research credit against payroll tax for small businesses · IRS: Form 6765 instructions

07FICA tip credit (now beyond restaurants)

How much
The employer Social Security and Medicare tax (7.65%) you pay on tips above the 2007 federal minimum wage of $5.15 an hour
Who
Restaurants and bars, and since 2025 other businesses where tipping is customary, such as salons, barbershops and spas
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 8846.

Source: Citrin Cooperman: law expands FICA tip credit beyond restaurants · IRS: Form 8846

Money you already paid

Taxes and payments you can get back.

08Fuel tax back on off-road equipment

How much
The federal fuel tax on fuel used off the road for business. IRS Publication 510 shows $0.243 a gallon for undyed diesel in unregistered construction vehicles
Who
Businesses that burn taxed fuel in equipment that isn't driven on public roads: construction equipment, generators, compressors, power saws, a landscaper's mowers and chainsaws. Not personal use or highway vehicles
How to get it
Claim it on IRS Form 4136 with your return, or quarterly on Form 8849. Keep fuel receipts and a log of which equipment used it.
Note
Example only: 10,000 gallons of undyed diesel × $0.243 = $2,430.

Source: IRS: fuel tax credit · IRS Publication 510

09Unclaimed money in your business's name

How much
Whatever is there: uncashed checks, deposits, credit balances and refunds that companies turned over to the state
Who
Any owner. Search your business name, old names and old addresses
How to get it
Search free at MissingMoney.com (most states) and your state's official unclaimed property site. Claiming from the state is free, so you don't need to pay a finder's fee.

Source: NAUPA (state treasurers): search for free · NAUPA: claiming vs. reporting

Grants, training money and rebates

Money you apply for. Amounts and rules vary by program and state.

10Training money for new hires and current staff

How much
On-the-job training: about 50% of a new hire's wages for up to 6 months. Training grants for current workers often cover up to 90% of direct training costs
Who
Employers hiring for permanent, full-time jobs that need training, or upskilling current employees. Rules vary by state
How to get it
Call your local American Job Center and ask for a business services representative. They handle the state and federal training funds.

Source: CareerOneStop (U.S. Dept. of Labor): training assistance for employers

11Energy and equipment rebates

How much
Varies by program: rebates and incentives for efficient lighting, HVAC, motors, solar and more
Who
Businesses in the program's state or utility area
How to get it
Search DSIRE (over 2,800 active incentives, run by N.C. State for the Energy Department) and your utility's business rebate page before you buy, since some programs need approval first.

Source: U.S. Department of Energy: DSIRE · DSIRE

12SBIR and STTR research grants

How much
Federal R&D awards; amounts vary by agency and phase
Who
Small U.S. businesses doing research that a federal agency wants (defense, energy, health, agriculture and others)
How to get it
Watch agency solicitations on SBIR.gov. Congress renewed the programs in April 2026 through September 30, 2031.

Source: SBA: grants · SBIR reauthorization status

13Export help through your state (STEP)

How much
Varies by state
Who
Small businesses starting or growing sales abroad
How to get it
SBA funds states to help small businesses export through the State Trade Expansion Program. Ask your state's trade office.

Source: SBA: grants

Watch list

Programs that are paused or changing. We cover them in the newsletter when they reopen.

Hiring credit (WOTC): lapsed for 2026 hires

The Work Opportunity Tax Credit (up to $2,400 for most hires, more for some veterans) only covers people who started work by December 31, 2025. Bills in Congress (H.R. 6231 and S. 3265) would bring it back through 2030, retroactive to January 1, 2026, but nothing has passed. If it does, ask your payroll provider how to file for 2026 hires.

Source: WOTC status tracker

USDA rural energy grants (REAP): new rules, no window yet

A rule effective October 16, 2026 says rural small businesses must finish and run a project before applying, with 12 months of energy data before and after. USDA will announce application windows later.

Source: Federal Register: REAP rule, Oct 1, 2026
